The Unseen Attack

Spontaneous Coronary Artery Dissection (SCAD) is a leading cause of heart attacks in young women. Yet, it remains dangerously under-diagnosed and under-researched.

It accounts for 35% of heart attacks in women under 50.

The Patient

SCAD challenges the stereotype of a heart attack patient, which is a primary reason it's often missed.

SCAD is a leading cause of heart attacks in women under 50

While often overlooked, SCAD is a primary cause of heart attacks in women under 50, making it a critical issue in young women's health.

SCAD accounts for 35% of heart attacks in this demographic.

Source: American College of Cardiology (2025a)

Over 90% of SCAD Patients are Women

The Left Anterior Descending (LAD) is Most Commonly Affected

Known as the "widowmaker" artery, the LAD is the site of dissection in over half of all SCAD cases.

Source: Prakash et al. (2021)

The Treatment Decision

Once SCAD is diagnosed, a critical decision follows. Unlike typical heart attacks, the approach is more nuanced, balancing intervention with the body's ability to heal.

Complete Blockages (Type 4) Are More Likely to Receive a Stent

The type of tear dictates the treatment. Doctors are more likely to intervene with a stent (PCI) for a complete blockage.

Source: Prakash et al. (2021)

Stenting Commits Patients to More Aggressive Medication

Receiving a stent requires a more intense regimen of blood thinners (DAPT) to prevent clotting.

Source: Prakash et al. (2021)

The Long-Term Outlook

For survivors, the journey continues with long-term risks and considerations.

1 in 5 Survivors Will Have Another SCAD Event

The risk of recurrence is a major concern for patients. Out of 20 survivors, 4 may experience another dissection.

(15-22% reported recurrence rate in major studies)

Source: Hayes et al. (2018)

The Algorithmic Double-Edged Sword

Artificial Intelligence holds immense promise for solving the SCAD diagnostic puzzle, but it also risks amplifying the very biases that harm patients today.

The Promise of AI

  • 1

    Enhanced Analysis

    AI can recognize subtle SCAD patterns in angiograms that are often missed by the human eye, reducing misdiagnosis.

  • 2

    Safer Diagnosis

    By improving non-invasive CCTA scans, AI could help many patients avoid higher-risk invasive procedures altogether.

  • 3

    Personalized Risk Prediction

    AI can integrate thousands of data points to predict who is at highest risk, enabling preventive care.

The Peril of Bias

  • 1

    Biased Training Data

    If AI is trained on historical data that underrepresents women, it will be less accurate for the primary SCAD population.

  • 2

    The "Common Disease" Trap

    An AI may learn to ignore rare diseases like SCAD to optimize its accuracy for more common heart attacks.

  • 3

    Automating Misdiagnosis

    A biased AI could lead to widespread, automated diagnostic errors, amplifying existing health inequities.

Source: The Algorithmic Double-Edged Sword: AI, Gender Bias, and the Diagnostic Challenge of SCAD

The Cascade of Bias in AI-Powered SCAD Diagnosis

Stage of Bias Mechanism Consequence for SCAD Patient
Historical Research Bias Systemic exclusion of women from cardiovascular trials, leading to a male-centric view of heart disease. SCAD, as a predominantly female and non-atherosclerotic condition, remains under-researched and under-prioritized.
Data Collection Bias AI training datasets (EHRs, trial data) reflect historical biases and lack demographic and disease diversity. High-quality, labeled SCAD cases are rare. The AI model lacks sufficient data to learn the distinct patterns of SCAD. Its "worldview" is skewed toward common, male-pattern heart attacks.
Algorithmic Training Bias Model is optimized for overall accuracy, leading it to favor the most common diagnosis (atherosclerosis) and ignore rare exceptions. The algorithm becomes statistically and functionally blind to SCAD, programmed to achieve high performance by ignoring the rare but life-threatening case.
Clinical Deployment Bias A clinician receives an AI-generated report that carries a "veneer of objectivity," influencing their decision-making. An AI confidently misclassifies a SCAD case as "mild atherosclerosis," leading to inappropriate treatment and potential iatrogenic harm. The misdiagnosis is automated and scaled.

Navigating the Future: The Path to a Responsible AI

Fair Data

Actively build diverse, high-quality SCAD patient registries, focusing on the inclusion of women and underrepresented groups. The foundation of a fair AI is fair data.

Transparent Algorithms

Prioritize "Explainable AI" (XAI) that allows clinicians to understand *why* an algorithm made a recommendation, ensuring trust and accountability.

The Human in the Loop

Frame AI as a powerful "co-pilot" to augment, not replace, the expert clinician, ensuring data is balanced with wisdom and empathy.

By tackling the difficult case of SCAD correctly, we can forge the tools and best practices necessary to ensure AI is deployed safely and equitably across all of medicine, turning a diagnostic challenge into a blueprint for a more intelligent and equitable future in cardiology.

A Call for Awareness

The data tells a clear story: SCAD is not a rare curiosity but a significant and under-recognized cause of heart attacks, particularly in a demographic that is too often overlooked in cardiology. It is a condition defined by the unexpected—affecting the young, the healthy, and overwhelmingly, women. Moving forward requires a fundamental shift: from dismissing the "atypical" patient to recognizing the unique signature of SCAD. By raising awareness, investing in targeted research, and educating both clinicians and the public, we can ensure that this "unseen attack" is finally brought into the light.
